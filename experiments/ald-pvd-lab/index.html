MATERIALS SCIENCE / THIN FILMS

Thin film deposition: ALD vs PVD

Two ways to put an ultra-thin film on a surface — one grows by self-limiting chemistry, the other by ballistic flight in vacuum. This lab turns the three ideas that are hardest to learn from text into things you can push, break, and predict.

The setup

THE PROBLEM

Chip makers, optics labs, and medical-device fabs all face the same task: deposit a film that is nanometers thick, and make it the same thickness everywhere — including down inside deep, narrow holes. Two tool families do this:

ALD

Atomic Layer Deposition

Alternate two gases. Each gas reacts with the surface until the surface is full, then stops. Repeat.

PVD

Physical Vapor Deposition

Knock atoms off a solid target (sputtering) or boil them off (evaporation). They fly in straight lines and stick where they land.

THE THREE HARD IDEAS

  1. Self-limitation. Why "more gas" stops meaning "more film" in ALD.
  2. Line-of-sight vs. everywhere. Why PVD starves trench bottoms and ALD doesn't.
  3. Growth curves that bend. Why real thickness vs. cycles isn't a perfect line.

Each lab below attacks exactly one of these.

LAB 01 / SELF-LIMITING REACTIONS

One cycle at a time

ALD grows film one cycle at a time. A cycle has four steps: pulse precursor A (it sticks only where there are open surface sites), purge (sweep out leftovers), pulse precursor B (it reacts only with what A left behind), purge. The punchline to feel: once the surface is saturated, more precursor cannot push more growth.

Predict first

You run a cycle with the normal A-dose and the surface saturates: film grows 0.10 nm this cycle. Next cycle you triple the A-dose (and keep everything else). The film grows…

The answer is ~0.10 nm. A's sticking is self-limiting: A binds only to open sites, and an A-covered site rejects more A. Saturated is saturated — extra dose just leaves in the purge. This is why ALD thickness is set by a count (cycles), not a knob (time): d = Ncycles × GPC. Surface coverage model simplified

A Langmuir-type fit for "how full is the surface after exposure D". Real ALD saturation curves look like this plateau, though actual chemistry adds steric crowding, so a "full" surface is usually sub-monolayer.

Words you just used

Half-reaction
One self-limiting surface reaction (A-pulse or B-pulse). ALD = two half-reactions back to back.
Purge
Inert gas sweep between pulses so the precursors never meet in the gas phase. No purge = CVD-like parasitic gas reaction = particles and dust.
Saturation
The dose beyond which more precursor adds nothing. The plateau on the left chart.

Lab 1 takeaway

ALD is digital: count cycles to set thickness. The dose slider only decides how close to full the surface gets — above the saturation point, extra precursor is money burned in the pump. Temperature sets how completely each half-reaction finishes (Lab 3 will zoom in on this).

LAB 02 / LINE-OF-SIGHT VS. EVERYWHERE

The trench test

Real devices coat deep holes (vias, trenches, 3D transistors). PVD atoms travel in straight lines in vacuum — they land where they can "see", and shadow everything else. ALD precursors are gases: they fill the whole chamber, reach every surface, and react until full — everywhere at once. Drag the aspect ratio and watch the bottom of the trench starve — or not.

Predict first

You're PVD-sputtering a trench whose depth is 10× its width. The film on the trench bottom currently reaches 40% of the film on the field (the flat top). You then make the trench twice as deep at the same width. Step coverage (bottom ÷ field) becomes…

Much lower. The bottom only sees the narrow cone of near-vertical trajectories that fit through the mouth. Halve the angular window and the flux drops steeply — step coverage collapses toward zero as aspect ratio (depth ÷ width) grows. Meanwhile ALD's step coverage stays near 100% because the gas is the flux: every surface site gets saturated per cycle whether it's on the field or the trench floor. Step coverage the industry metric

SC = tbottom / tfield × 100%

Sometimes quoted at the sidewall midpoint instead of the bottom. 100% = perfectly conformal. PVD in high-aspect features can sit at a few percent; ALD is prized precisely because SC ≈ 100%.

PVD arrival rate simplified

R ∝ J · s · coskθ · visible?

Flux J from the source, sticking coefficient s, an angular weighting, and — the killer — whether the point can see the source at all. Our sim ray-casts straight lines against the trench wall to compute exactly this visibility.

Why ALD doesn't care

θ(local) → 1 everywhere

Pressure is high enough that gas molecules collide far more often with walls than with each other (Knudsen transport), so precursor floods the feature. Each surface site self-limits at its own saturation dose. simplified We show instant gas access; at extreme aspect ratios (≳50:1) and low pressure, even ALD can lag inside features.

Lab 2 takeaway

PVD is ballistic: beautiful on flat wafers, optics, and large features; it starves anything it can't "see". ALD is conformal by construction — the reason it conquered high-κ gates, 3D NAND word lines, and GAA nanosheets. The cost: ALD is usually 10–100× slower (cycles with purges), which is why both families live side by side in a fab.

LAB 03 / REAL GROWTH CURVES BEND

Nucleation delay & the temperature window

"Thickness = cycles × GPC" is the mental model. Two real effects bend that line: on a surface that doesn't offer the right chemistry, the first cycles form isolated islands (nothing measurable yet), and temperature must sit inside a window — too cold and the half-reactions stall, too hot and the precursor desorbs or decomposes (sneaking back toward CVD). Scrub the sliders and watch the curves deform.

Predict first

A recipe gives exactly 1.0 nm after 10 cycles on a fresh SiO2 wafer (GPC = 0.10 nm). On a bare platinum surface — same chamber, same recipe — you measure only 0.4 nm after the same 10 cycles. The most likely explanation:

Nucleation delay. Many ALD chemistries "want" surface –OH groups. SiO2 has them; freshly cleaned Pt mostly doesn't. The first cycles find only scattered islands, so average thickness lags the ideal line until the islands coalesce — the curve shows an incubation period. Watch it happen in the left chart below by switching the surface to inert. (This effect is also a feature: it underlies area-selective ALD.) Purges remove gas-phase precursor, not film — and reflection is a PVD intuition, not an ALD one.

ALD with incubation simplified

d = GPC × max(0, N − Ndelay)

Ndelay ≈ 0 on reactive surfaces; roughly 5–20 cycles on inert ones. Real curves round off smoothly (island growth → coalescence) rather than kinking sharply.

PVD stays simple

d = R × t

Rate R (nm/min) is set by source power/temperature and geometry. Linear from t = 0 in this model. simplified Real PVD also has nucleation and grain growth — we ignore them here to keep the comparison crisp.

Why a window exists

GPC(T) ≈ const only inside [Tlow, Thigh]

Too cold: reactions stall — ligands don't fully leave, GPC drops and film quality suffers. Too hot: precursor desorbs before reacting, or decomposes (non-self-limiting, CVD-like creep up). The plateau is the whole reason ALD is predictable.

Lab 3 takeaway

The straight line d = N × GPC is earned, not given: you need a reactive surface (or accept incubation / use it on purpose for selectivity) and a temperature inside the window. When a production ALD process drifts, these are the two suspects process engineers interrogate first.

Simplification ledger read me

Every simulation here is a teaching cartoon. The list below flags each place where we cheated, so you know which intuitions transfer and which don't.

  • 2D cross-sections. Real trenches are 3D; corner and pattern-density effects are omitted.
  • Point molecules. Real precursors are bulky; steric crowding means a "full" cycle is typically a fraction of a true monolayer (GPC < 1 ML).
  • Instant gas transport. We flood features instantly. In reality, transport inside extreme-aspect-ratio features can limit ALD too (lower pressure, longer purges, higher temperature all help).
  • Temperature is qualitative. The window shown (≈150–300 °C) is representative of thermal ALD chemistries like Al2O3; plasma (PE-)ALD and other chemistries have different windows.
  • Ballistic PVD only. No re-sputtering, no surface diffusion of adatoms, no resputter-fill of undercut — real PVD fills better (and worse) than our pure line-of-sight cartoon in different regimes.
  • Nucleation is caricatured as a hard delay; real island growth gives a smooth, chemistry-specific curve.
  • No chemistry, no heat, no flow. Real reactors have gas-phase dynamics, ligand exchange energetics, and heat/mass transfer — captured here only in their qualitative consequences.

Mini-glossary

GPC
Growth per cycle (Å or nm per ALD cycle). The "step height" of digital growth.
Half-reaction
One self-limiting precursor–surface reaction.
Purge
Inert-gas sweep preventing gas-phase reactions between precursors.
Step coverage
Thickness inside a feature ÷ thickness on the open field.
Conformal
Same thickness on every surface, regardless of orientation.
Line-of-sight
Ballistic transport: straight trajectories, geometric shadowing.
Knudsen transport
Gas-flow regime where molecules collide with walls more than each other — gas creeps into features.
Incubation / nucleation delay
Early cycles of reduced growth on surfaces lacking anchor sites.
ALD window
Temperature range where GPC is roughly constant and both half-reactions complete.
CVD
Chemical Vapor Deposition: continuous co-flow of precursors — faster, less conformal, not self-limiting. ALD's ancestor and competitor.
