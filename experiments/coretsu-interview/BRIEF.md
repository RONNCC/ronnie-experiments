# Coretsu — last-minute interview brief
Research checked November 1, 2026. Independent preparation, not company-endorsed.

## Say this in 30 seconds
“Coretsu describes applied technology for the U.S. government, with deployments at the Pentagon, Congress, and OMB. The FedRAMP listing describes AI-enhanced access to public government information using APIs. I'd like to understand which user workflows this team owns and how it measures trustworthy outcomes.”

## Know the evidence boundaries
- **Company claim:** deployments are stated on https://www.coretsu.com/; their scope is not disclosed.
- **Government record:** https://www.fedramp.gov/marketplace/products/FR2602662279/ lists Coretsu Government Suite, Initial Implementation, Agency Auth In Process (June 8, 2026), zero authorizations, no certified-since date. Do not call it certified.
- **Founder reporting:** Alex Handy is identified as founder in Bloomberg Government reporting republished at https://mehlmanconsulting.com/news/k-street-ai-spending-boom-fueled-by-startups-and-big-tech/.
- **Funding database:** https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/1404957-79 reports a $38M seed dated May 22, 2025 and HOF Capital as an investor. Not independently company-confirmed here.
- **Entity:** UEI U77VUFCUNLJ3 in FedRAMP matches https://govtribe.com/vendors/coretsu-inc-dot-14za2. Registration is not an award.
- **Unknown:** revenue, team size, underlying models, role requirements, interview loop, detailed customer usage. Do not infer ownership from professional affiliations.

## Bring three real stories
1. Ambiguity: what you learned, the reversible first step, and the outcome.
2. Ownership: what you personally shipped or improved and how you measured it.
3. Failure/disagreement: your contribution, how you corrected course, and what changed.

## Technical talking points (hypothetical, not their stack)
For an evidence-backed policy brief: clarify jurisdiction and dates; retrieve authoritative versioned records; distinguish introduced from enacted policy; use typed API tools; cite supporting passages; expose incomplete coverage; constrain tools against prompt injection; evaluate missed changes, false changes, citation support, latency, and review burden. Start read-only and require review for consequential actions.

## Ask them
- Which workflow would I own, and what would success at 90 days look like?
- What separates a successful demo from a sustained deployment here?
- How does the current authorization boundary shape delivery?
- How do you measure factual quality and respond to incorrect answers?
- What can you share about team structure, funding/runway, and repeatable product versus custom work?
