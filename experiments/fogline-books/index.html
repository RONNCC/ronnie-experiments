CHAPTER A · FOUNDATIONS

What a ledger actually does

Double-entry bookkeeping, the chart of accounts, and why a balanced ledger can still be economically wrong.

01FOUNDATIONS

“Where did the money come from?”

ledgeraccountsdouble-entryassetsliabilitiesequityrevenueexpenses

Drag a business event onto the ledger by building both sides of its entry. Debits and credits are just the two sides of an entry — not “bad/good”, and total debits must equal total credits. Pick an event, choose a debit and a credit, then post.

Predict first

Fogline borrows $10,000 from a bank. Cash rises by $10,000 — so profit rises by $10,000 too. True or false?

False. The entry is Debit Cash / Credit Loan payable — an asset and a liability rise together. No revenue, no expense, no profit. Cash received is not necessarily revenue. Try posting it below — then try posting it as revenue on purpose and watch the two meters disagree.

CHAPTER C · TIMING

Cash, invoices, and earned revenue

Cash moves when cash moves. Revenue is earned when obligations are satisfied. The gap between the two is where accrual accounting lives.

06TIMING

“Cash received isn't always revenue earned”

Under accrual, the $12,000 payment creates cash + a liability, and each month moves $1,000 from liability to revenue via an adjusting entry. Prepaid insurance is the mirror: cash + an asset, amortized into expense. Timing differences made visible — that's what adjusting entries are for.

The challenge

The customer pays the final $2,000. Does Fogline record another $2,000 of revenue?

In this accrual example the $3,000 of revenue was earned when the invoice was issued for work already delivered. Every collection after that is just swapping one asset (receivable) for another (cash). Treating collections as new revenue double-counts.

Demo 7 takeaway

Receivables and payables are timing bridges between economic events and cash. Revenue/expense hit when the obligation is created or satisfied; cash movements after that only settle balances.

    §APPENDICES

    Simplifications, glossary & sources

    📓 Simplification ledger read me

    • Single-currency, tax-free world. No sales tax, VAT, withholding, or FX — real books have all four.
    • Straight-line everything. Even delivery, even amortization; real contracts have ramps, usage, and variable consideration.
    • Toy ledger engine. No periods in the journal itself, no multi-currency remeasurement, no audit immutability beyond Demo 12's log.
    • Illustrative model scores. “Confidence 0.92” and Demo 15's dataset are invented to teach policy design, not model evaluation.
    • Cash-flow statement is skeletal. A simplified indirect-method sketch, not a full reconciliation.
    • Consolidation is partial. Full elimination of one loan + one service charge; no NCI, no goodwill, no FX translation.
    • Firm economics are caricatures. Demo 16's hours and fees illustrate scope-sensitivity, not any real firm's unit economics.

    📖 Mini-glossary

    Debit / credit
    The left / right side of a journal entry. Whether a debit increases an account depends on its type (assets/expenses vs liabilities/equity/revenue).
    Chart of accounts
    The named list of accounts a company posts to — ideally with stable IDs and semantic roles behind the labels.
    Accrual vs cash
    Accrual records events when obligations arise/are satisfied; cash records when money moves.
    Deferred revenue
    Cash collected for service not yet delivered — a liability, not revenue.
    Receivable / payable
    Amounts owed to / by the company — timing bridges between events and cash.
    Reconciliation
    Explaining every difference between two records (e.g., bank vs books); booking only what's genuinely missing.
    Consolidation
    Presenting a group as one economic entity by eliminating intragroup balances and transactions.
    Idempotency key
    A client-supplied token letting a server recognize retries and return the original result instead of re-executing.
    Materiality
    Whether an error is big enough to change a decision — the lens that turns accuracy into operating policy.
