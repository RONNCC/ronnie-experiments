01 / USE

Where it fits

Stroke and systemic embolism prevention in nonvalvular atrial fibrillation; DVT and PE treatment and prevention of recurrence; and postoperative DVT prophylaxis after hip or knee replacement.

For acute VTE treatment, the usual adult lead-in is 10 mg twice daily for 7 days, then 5 mg twice daily. Extended prevention has a separate regimen.

02 / DOSE

When less is more

For nonvalvular AF, reduce from 5 mg to 2.5 mg twice daily when at least two are present: age ≥80 years, weight ≤60 kg, or serum creatinine ≥1.5 mg/dL. This rule is not the VTE treatment dosing rule.

03 / SAFETY

Bleeding & procedures

Bleeding is the principal risk. For life-threatening or uncontrolled bleeding, andexanet alfa is a specific reversal agent; 4-factor PCC may be considered according to local protocols.

Label guidance: hold at least 24 h for low-risk procedures, and 48 h for moderate/high bleeding risk. Individualize for renal function, procedure and thrombosis risk with the treating team.

02 / THE PARADOX LUPUS ANTICOAGULANT

Longer in the tube.
Clots in the body.

A name that seems to contradict itself. Lupus anticoagulant is an antibody pattern that interferes with phospholipid-dependent clotting assays, yet is associated with thrombosis in vivo.

THE CENTRAL IDEA

In vitro: prolonged clotting.
In vivo: thrombotic risk.

PHOSPHOLIPID SURFACE
IN VITRO / ASSAY INTERFERENCE

A competition for surface.

Antibodies can bridge β₂-glycoprotein I on phospholipid surfaces, interfering with the phospholipid-dependent assembly of clotting factors and prolonging the test.

DECODING THE NAME

Not necessarily lupus.
Not protective.

LA is a functional pattern caused by antiphospholipid antibodies, often directed against phospholipid-binding proteins including β₂-glycoprotein I and prothrombin. It can occur without systemic lupus.

THE APS CONNECTION

Antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) combines appropriate clinical features with persistent laboratory evidence: LA, anticardiolipin IgG or IgM, or anti-β₂GPI IgG or IgM. The 2006 Sydney classification criteria require persistence on repeat testing at least 12 weeks apart; classification criteria are not a substitute for clinical diagnosis.

THE DETAILS MATTER

Pre-analytics & other pitfalls

01 / Acute phase CRP may interfere with some aPTT reagents; acute illness can complicate interpretation.

02 / Factor deficiency Low factors can prolong screening tests and may correct on mixing. A mix is not definitive by itself.

03 / Sample quality Correctly fill the citrate tube to maintain the blood-to-anticoagulant ratio and prepare double-spun platelet-poor plasma per lab protocol.

Testing is best performed off interfering anticoagulation when clinically safe, or using validated DOAC removal. Never interrupt therapy solely for testing without the treating clinician's plan.
