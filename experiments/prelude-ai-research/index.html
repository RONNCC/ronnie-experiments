What Prelude is doing

The honest answer: the public record is thin, and that appears intentional. The only first-party description is Maduka's own LinkedIn post from January 2026:

"For the past few months, I've been quietly building something at the intersection of AI and financial services. We're a small, tight-knit team (4 people + some AI homies) that's shipping fast and learning faster. We're looking for 1-2 more applied AI engineers to join us. The work is hard, the scope is wide, the problems are unique, and the opportunity is large."

Read together with the founder's background (a decade of fintech product work, most recently leading Plaid's core bank-data connectivity products), the plausible shape is an AI product aimed at financial-services workflows - but the specific wedge, customer, and product are not public. The domain prelude.ai was registered to the venture but returns no public site at the time of writing.

A February 2026 Stealth Startup Spy newsletter entry - "Ex-GM and Head of Product at Plaid with prior leadership at Glassdoor, NerdWallet, and Citi enters stealth" - matches Maduka's resume line for line, which independently corroborates that he left Plaid to found a company. Early life
Born in Nigeria; family immigrated to the US when he was young. Studied electrical engineering at Princeton. (Source: Arcadea Group interview, 2022)
~2008
Started his career as a mortgage trader at Citi - "at the onset of the 2008 financial crisis," as he describes it. Began angel investing with colleagues while there.
After Citi
Harvard Business School, then moved from finance into tech.
NerdWallet

Glassdoor
Led product teams on job-search and career problems.
Plaid
Product leader for core data aggregation products - the bank-connectivity APIs that much of US fintech is built on. Public profiles describe him at GM / Head of Product level. Left to found Prelude (CA incorporation filed August 2025).
Investing
Angel investor since his Citi years; member of Harlem Capital's inaugural angel program cohort (2021), a program backing operators who support diverse founders.

What he has said about how he works

  • On product leadership: measure "decision making quality over time," keep shipping cadence high, learn from customers directly.
  • On early teams: "One major mistake is building too big of a product team too early" - consistent with Prelude staying at ~4 people.
  • On APIs as products: reliability, quality, and security are the bar when customers build businesses on top of you - relevant context for whatever Prelude ships into financial services.

Why this founder-problem fit matters

Plaid's data aggregation layer sits underneath most consumer fintech. Someone who ran that product sees - at scale - where financial data flows break, where compliance friction lives, and which workflows banks and fintechs still do manually. Those are exactly the seams an applied-AI product in financial services would target. That is inference from his resume, not a statement from the company.

Questions a first conversation should answer

  • The wedge: who is the first customer, and which financial-services workflow is AI taking over end-to-end versus assisting?
  • The role: what does an applied AI engineer own - evals and guardrails, the product surface, data pipelines - and what does success look like in the first 90 days?
  • Trust and compliance: in a regulated industry, how is the team thinking about auditability, data lineage, and model-risk review? (A fair question to pose to a founder who ran infrastructure other companies depend on.)
  • Runway and plans: funding status, and when does the company come out of stealth?
  • The team: who else is building, and why is this the group that wins this problem?
  • The codename: what is lasagna? (Layers, presumably. Worth asking.)
