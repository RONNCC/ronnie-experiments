DEMO C / PRECOMPUTED, REPRESENTATIVE RTL — NOT TOOL OUTPUT

Same C loop, four different circuits.

High-level synthesis (HLS) tools compile C/C++ into RTL, guided by pragmas — directives that say “unroll this loop”, “pipeline this loop”. Toggle the pragmas and compare the C source with representative RTL a tool would generate. These RTL variants are precomputed teaching sketches that capture each tool's structural choice (adders, registers, scheduling); exact output differs by tool and version.

Disclosed toy schedule and limits

Latency uses a deliberately simple schedule: loop passes + 2 cycles of fill/drain overhead; with the pipeline pragma, initiation interval (II) is 1 — one element enters per cycle — and latency is N + 2. Real tools compute exact schedules from their own schedulers and report them in their reports; use those numbers for real work. “Area” here counts adders plus a half-unit for pipeline registers, nothing more.

DEMO D / SIMPLIFIED ESTIMATES — NOT A QUOTE

What does one good die cost?

Move the sliders to see how die size, wafer price, defect density, and program NRE (design + masks + tooling) combine. The model: gross dies per wafer from the standard wafer-area approximation; yield from a Poisson defect model; cost per good die = wafer price ÷ good dies; NRE amortized across your volume. Real foundry quotes, yields, and NRE are private and vary widely — treat every number here as an order-of-magnitude estimate.

Disclosed model, public price ranges, and where the numbers come from

Gross dies per wafer ≈ π·(d/2)²/A − π·d/√(2A) with a 300 mm wafer (the standard approximation that subtracts edge loss). Yield = e−D·A: a Poisson model assuming defects are scattered independently — real defects cluster, and real yields are foundry secrets, so this is optimistic on big dies. Wafer-price and NRE presets are midpoints of publicly reported ranges (see the sources section); foundries do not publish prices. NRE here means the whole program: engineering, EDA tools, IP licenses, and masks. The most complex leading-edge SoCs are reported to run far past these midpoints.
