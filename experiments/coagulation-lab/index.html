STATION 00 / PREANALYTICS — WHERE MOST ERRORS HAPPEN

The citrate tube: freezing time without freezing biology

Coagulation tests don’t run on serum — they need plasma with clotting factors still intact, but not yet clotted. Sodium citrate (in the light-blue cap) does that trick reversibly: it chelates Ca²⁺. No free calcium → cascade pauses. In the analyzer we add calcium back to restart the clock under controlled conditions. Get the blood-to-citrate ratio wrong, and you’ve diluted the factors or left calcium around — either way the times lie.

Predict first

A nervous phlebotomist draws only half a tube — the citrate tube is 50% full. What happens to PT and aPTT?

Longer. Tube holds a fixed puff of citrate meant for 1 part citrate + 9 parts blood. Underfill = too much citrate per mL of plasma. When the analyzer adds its standard CaCl₂ dose, some calcium is mopped up by the extra citrate and free Ca²⁺ is low → every Ca-dependent step slows. This is the #1 preanalytical reject. Twin culprit: high hematocrit — RBCs displace plasma so the same citrate is dissolved in less plasma.

Station 00 takeaway

Citrate doesn’t remove calcium — it hides it. The analyzer’s first job is to give it back, precisely, so clotting starts at time zero. Everything after is just “how long until fibrin appears under defined trigger.” If the tube is wrong, the story is wrong before page 1.

STATION 01 / THE CLOT TIMER

PT/INR & aPTT: same detector, different starter pistol

Both tests ask one question: how many seconds from trigger to visible fibrin? They differ only in what they trigger. PT hammers the extrinsic + common arm with tissue factor (thromboplastin) — think scraped vessel wall. aPTT tiptoes via contact activation + phospholipid (no tissue factor) to interrogate the intrinsic + common arm. Neither is a “global coagulation test” — each is blind to the other arm.

Predict first

You spike normal plasma with therapeutic enoxaparin (LMW heparin, anti-Xa). Which times move?

aPTT moves a little, PT hardly at all. Unfractionated heparin (anti-IIa + anti-Xa) potently prolongs aPTT; low-molecular-weight heparin is engineered to be more anti-Xa-selective and often leaves even aPTT insensitive — labs use anti-Xa activity instead. This is why “monitoring heparin with aPTT vs anti-Xa” is an assay-choice debate, not just a target debate. Try the reagent bench below.

What PT & aPTT can’t tell you

In vitro times ≠ in vivo bleeding/thrombosis risk. Lupus anticoagulant prolongs aPTT yet clots. Factor XII deficiency prolongs aPTT with no bleeding. Always interpret with D-dimer, fibrinogen, platelets, and story.

Station 01 takeaway

Think pistol + stopwatch: PT fires tissue factor; aPTT fires contact + phospholipid. Both stop when fibrin scatters light. INR is just a unit conversion so warfarin dose travels between labs.

STATION 02 / HOW MUCH FIBRIN CAN YOU MAKE?

Fibrinogen (Clauss) & thrombin time: weighing the mesh

PT/aPTT tell you how fast fibrin appears. Fibrinogen asks how much. In Clauss fibrinogen, the lab overwhelms all upstream regulation by flooding diluted plasma with excess thrombin — clot time now reflects only fibrinogen concentration. Flip it: in thrombin time (TT), we ask “if I give you thrombin, how long until you clot?” — a sensitive sniff for anything blocking that last step.

Station 02 takeaway

PT tells speed; fibrinogen tells material. You can have a normal PT but bleed from afibrinogenemia, and you can have a sky-high Clauss (acute phase) while DIC is already chewing through platelets — look at the whole panel.

STATION 03 / LEFTOVERS

D-dimer: not a clot test — a cleanup test

D-dimer doesn’t detect clot — it detects what’s left after clot is built, cross-linked by factor XIIIa, then chopped by plasmin. Only cross-linked fibrin yields the D-dimer neoepitope; fibrinogen chopped alone does not. Labs measure it with latex immuno-turbidimetry: antibody-coated beads agglutinate when D-dimer bridges them → light scattering ↑ → absorbance change converted via calibrators. High sensitivity, terrible specificity — which is the point for rule-out.

Units matter

1 DDU ≈ 2 FEU

FEU = fibrinogen-equivalent units (whole fibrinogen mass); DDU = D-dimer mass only. Mixing them moves the cutoff 2×. Always check which your lab reports.

Why sensitivity > specificity?

  • Negative highly sensitive assay → good rule-out in low pre-test probability (e.g., Wells low PE).
  • Many things raise D-dimer: clot, trauma, surgery, cancer, infection, pregnancy, age, inflammation.
  • Not for screening the well. Use only with validated clinical decision rule.

Station 03 takeaway

D-dimer is a fibrinolysis receipt, not a thrombosis photo. Its job is to say “no recent cross-linked fibrin cleanup? probably no clot to clean” — and only when pretest probability already made that plausible.

STATION 04 / CORRECTION LOGIC

Mixing studies: does normal plasma fix it?

Prolonged aPTT or PT? Is it not enough factor or something blocking the reaction? Mix 1:1 with normal pooled plasma (NPP) — which has ~100% of every factor — and re-time. A deficiency gets topped up past the ~30% hemostatic threshold → time corrects. An inhibitor (antibody, drug, lupus anticoagulant) poisons the mix too → fails to correct. Lupus vs specific factor inhibitor needs incubated mixes (progressive FVIII antibodies need time/temperature).

Predict first

aPTT 78 s (ref 25–35). 1:1 mix immediately → 44 s; after 2 h incubation at 37 °C → 68 s. Interpretation?

Time-dependent inhibitor, classic for acquired FVIII autoantibody. Lupus anticoagulant is immediate and persistent. Simple deficiency corrects and stays corrected. Heparin effect disappears with heparinase, not time. This is why labs run both immediate and incubated mixes.

Pitfalls before you call it

  • Heparin contamination → prolonged that “corrects” with heparinase, not mixing.
  • DOACs → aPTT/PT/dRVVT prolongation mimicking LA — know drug history, use DOAC-adsorb.
  • Lupus anticoagulant antibodies are phospholipid-dependent — mixing barely moves the time because antibody remains in mix.

Station 04 takeaway

Correction = deficiency (topped up); non-correction = inhibitor (still there). Immediate vs incubated tells you whether that inhibitor needs time to bite — a clue that changes therapy from factor replacement to bypassing agents/immunosuppression.

STATION 05 / THE PHOSPHOLIPID PARADOX

Lupus anticoagulant: when longer clotting means higher clotting risk

Lupus anticoagulant (LA) is a misnomer: a family of autoantibodies against phospholipid-binding proteins (β₂-glycoprotein I, prothrombin). In a tube with limiting phospholipid, they steal the surface coagulation complexes need → longer clotting times. In a person, they activate endothelium/platelets/complement → thrombosis and pregnancy loss. Diagnosis is functional, not antigen: a three-step algorithm on two assay principles.

ISTH/Denver 3-step — screen → mix → confirm (×2 assay types)

Why two assay types?

No single assay catches all LA antibodies. ISTH requires two different principles (e.g., dRVVT + silica-based aPTT). dRVVT is more specific; aPTT-LA picks up additional antibodies. Confirm step is phospholipid-neutralisation — not “repeat the test”.

Antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) vs LA

  • LA = functional clotting assay (this bench).
  • Anticardiolipin & anti-β₂-GPI = solid-phase immunoassays (ELISA/chemiluminescence). See next bench.
  • APS = clinical criteria (thrombosis/pregnancy morbidity) + persistent lab positivity (>12 wks).

Station 05 takeaway

Prolonged ≠ bleeding. LA prolongs because the tube starves for phospholipid the antibody hides; the body clots because the same antibody provokes cells and complement. That paradox is why APS is a top thrombophilia to rule out — and why anticoagulants ruin the very tests that detect it.

STATION 06 / PROTEIN WORK — THREE DIFFERENT TRICKS

Thrombophilia proteins: chromogenic, clot-based & immune

Inherited thrombophilia often means not enough inhibitor — antithrombin, protein C, protein S — or too much driver that won’t turn off (factor VIII, APC resistance). Labs don’t count molecules one way: they steal the protein’s job and time it (chromogenic/clot-based activity), count its heads (antigen/latex), or see if activated protein C can still do its cut (APC resistance). Results only mean anything off acute thrombosis and off anticoagulants.

Timing matters

Acute thrombosis consumes AT/PC/PS and raises FVIII/fibrinogen → false ↓ or ↑. Estrogen, liver disease, DIC, VKA, DOACs all interfere. Guidelines: test remote (≥3 months post-event, off anticoagulants if safe) and repeat abnormal.

Antiphospholipid immunoassays

Anticardiolipin IgG/IgM and anti-β₂-GPI IgG/IgM are ELISA/chemiluminescent antigen assays (not clotting). Wells coated with cardiolipin-β₂-GPI complex capture autoantibody; HRP conjugate → TMB color. Positive = isotype titer + confirmation >12 weeks.

Station 06 takeaway

Activity = “does it work?”; antigen = “is it there?”; ratio = “can it be shut off?”. Chromogenic tricks use venom enzymes to bypass clotting; clot-based tricks lean into it; immune tricks count molecules. The clever bit is knowing which question the result actually answers — and what (pregnancy, pills, clot) is whispering the opposite.

STATION 07 / DNA — NOT A CLOTTING ASSAY

Factor V Leiden & prothrombin G20210A: reading the gene, not the clot

The two common inherited thrombophilia variants are single-nucleotide changes that raise thrombin generation for life: F5 c.1691G>A (Arg506Gln) — Factor V Leiden and F2 c.20210G>A (3′ UTR) — prothrombin mutation. Labs don’t infer them from times; they amplify DNA and spot the base — PCR with allele-specific probes, melt curves or targeted genotyping. Heparin in the tube doesn’t matter; what matters is whether you should test.

Station 07 takeaway

Genes don’t change; risk does. PCR tells you heterozygote vs homozygote — absolute context (age, hormones, surgery, family) tells you what, if anything, to do. DNA results are permanent; handle with counseling, not haste.

STATION 08 / ORDERING WISELY & TRAPS

Cases, caveats & when not to test

The biggest thrombophilia error isn’t a wrong reagent — it’s testing at the wrong time, then acting on noise. Anticoagulants, acute phase, pregnancy, estrogen and liver disease each systematically distort the panel. And in the modern DOAC era, the drug you gave can fake the disease you’re hunting. Use cases, not checkboxes.

Golden rules (ACCP/ASH/BCSH flavor)

  1. Don’t test everyone. First unprovoked VTE: testing rarely changes duration vs risk-score-guided decision; consider only if result alters counseling/family/anticoag choice.
  2. Timing: AT/PC/PS, LA — avoid acute thrombosis & acute illness (consume/produce). Repeat abnormals months later.
  3. Drug holiday: VKA ↓ PC/PS; DOACs prolong aPTT/PT/dRVVT & PS/PC clot-based & LA; heparin prolongs aPTT & interferes with AT. DNA unaffected.
  4. Family: Counsel before testing asymptomatic kin — what will you do with het FVL?

Viscoelastic outsider: TEG / ROTEM

Whole-blood, point-of-care: cup-and-pin measures clot strength & lysis in real time. Dominant in bleeding/transfusion algorithms (cardiac, trauma, obstetric) — not a thrombophilia diagnostic panel. Different instrument, different question.

Lab stewardship tip

Order: CBC + platelets + PT/INR + aPTT + fibrinogen + D-dimer for bleeding/clot workup; then targeted thrombophilia per indication. “Thrombophilia panel” as a shotgun order during acute VTE → mostly misleading.

Station 08 takeaway

Context is the reagent. The same number means opposite things before vs after heparin, on vs off the pill, day 1 vs month 4. DNA is stoic; nearly everything else is moody — respect the timing, name the interferent, and repeat the borderline.

Simplification ledger read me

Every bench above is a teaching cartoon. Here’s where we cheated, so you know which intuitions transfer.

  • Cartoon kinetics: Real coagulation is enzyme amplification with feedback (thrombin activates XI, VIII, V) and inhibition (TFPI, antithrombin, APC/PS, fibrinolysis). Timings are reagent-specific calibrations, not pure “factor % → seconds” math.
  • Reagent-specific cutoffs: All “normal ranges” here are illustrative (PT 10–13s, aPTT 25–35s, fibrinogen 200–400 mg/dL, D-dimer 0.5 FEU mg/L, etc.). Your lab’s reference interval and ISI/assay matter — never memorize hard cutoffs from a demo.
  • Detection: Labs use optical turbidity, mechanical (magnetic ball / viscosity / electromechanical), nephelometric and chemiluminescent detectors — curves simplified to single-threshold “time”.
  • LA diagnostics: ISTH guidelines require clinical correlation, two assay systems, mixing interpretation by % correction/index with lab-specific cutoffs, phospholipid neutralization, and persistence ≥12 weeks — we show the logic, not the full criteria audit.
  • Thrombophilia risk: Absolute VTE risk is multifactorial. Relative risks cited (~5× het FVL etc.) are population averages; individual risk depends on age, BMI, immobility, hormones, family, site of clot.
  • Genomics: PCR here is targeted single-variant testing; broad panels/sequencing for rare AT/PC/PS/FV/FVII/FX/FXII deficiencies are separate assays with variant interpretation.
  • Not included: Platelet function (PFA, LTA, VerifyNow), vWF workup, factor inhibitor Bethesda titers, thrombin generation, DIC scoring, cancer-associated clot workup — each its own lab universe.

Mini-glossary

PPP · platelet-poor plasma
Citrated plasma spun to <10k platelets/µL — platelets carry phospholipid & PF4 that muddy times if left.
TF · tissue factor (thromboplastin)
Transmembrane protein + phospholipid that allosterically activates VII → extrinsic entry.
ISI / MNPT
International Sensitivity Index & Mean Normal PT — thromboplastin calibration to make INR portable for VKA monitoring only.
Clauss vs PT-derived fibrinogen
Clauss = functional Clauss assay with excess thrombin & calibration (gold standard). PT-derived = estimate back-calculated from PT turbidity — imprecise.
FDP vs D-dimer
FDP = any fibrin(ogen) fragment; D-dimer = specifically cross-linked fibrin fragment with D-D neoepitope after FXIIIa + plasmin action.
FEU vs DDU
Fibrinogen-equivalent units vs D-dimer units; 1 DDU ≈ 2 FEU (mass basis). Cutoff mismatch hazard.
dRVVT
Dilute Russell viper venom time: venom directly activates X — LA-sensitive because it needs phospholipid; confirm step adds excess PL.
APC resistance
Failure of added activated protein C to prolong aPTT — functional readout most commonly due to FV Leiden (Arg506Gln) removing an APC cut site.
Chromogenic substrate
Short peptide linked to p-nitroaniline (pNA); enzyme cleavage releases yellow pNA — ΔA405 nm ∝ residual enzyme → inverse for inhibitor assays.
Bethesda
Not covered interactively: factor inhibitor titer (e.g., FVIII) — serial dilutions of patient into NPP, residual activity after incubation.
