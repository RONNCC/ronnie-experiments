Inside the Coagulation Lab

One requisition, eleven different benches. This walk-through opens each instrument, pipettes each reagent, and shows — molecules to signal — how the listed tests are actually run. Nothing here diagnoses anyone; every result card is a synthetic example for illustration only.

For a reader who remembers cells, proteins and DNA. We explain the machines themselves — optics vs mechanics, quadrupoles vs ELISA washers — not just their names. Where published methods are known we cite them; where labs vary, we label the method representative and name the alternative.

Not one tube for everything. The panel intentionally mixes specimen types:

  • Serum — Vitamin D; anticardiolipin & β2-glycoprotein I immunoassays in the APS panel
  • Citrated platelet-poor plasma (3.2% Na-citrate, 1:9) — all clot-based coag, chromogenic and antigen lactic tests
  • EDTA whole blood (or other validated DNA source) — Factor V Leiden PCR

Specimen containers and spin conditions vary by laboratory. The timings below are generic illustrations — hands-on vs instrument vs elapsed turnaround — not a claim about any specific lab’s queue.

TEST 01 / 11 · IMMUNOASSAY vs MASS SPECTROMETRY · SERUM

Vitamin D, 25-Hydroxy — separating D2 from D3

25-hydroxyvitamin D (calcidiol) is the circulating storage form that best reflects vitamin D status. The liver makes it from D3 (skin + diet) and D2 (plant/fungal ergocalciferol and supplements). Modern reference labs often report D2 and D3 separately plus a total (D2+D3). The reference measurement procedure is isotope-dilution LC-MS/MS [Mayo 25HDN: LC-MS/MS] [UW VITDG2: LC-MS/MS]. Many routine labs still use automated chemiluminescent immunoassay for speed — we show both and label which is which.

Specimen input (representative)

  • Container: serum — red-top (no additive) or serum-separator (SST) — spin after clot, aliquot to plastic vial.
  • Volume: 0.5 mL serum typical; 0.25 mL minimum in cited catalogue [Mayo sheet 0.5 mL / 0.25 min].
  • Not: plasma citrate — that tube is for coag benches, not here. Hemolyzed/lipemic may interfere with immunoassay more than LC-MS/MS.

DNA and coag protein tubes are different draws — see benches 02–10. One specimen-prep does not fit all.

Method class

LC-MS/MS gold standard — liquid chromatography separates D2/D3 and the 3-epi isomer; tandem mass spectrometry identifies by mass-to-charge (m/z). Representative immunoassay shown as alternative: competitive binding vs labeled D.

LC-MS/MS is the CDC Vitamin D Standardization-Certification reference [CDC VDSCP]. Immunoassays can cross-react with epimers and be biased by matrix — cited in method-comparison literature [LC-MS/MS vs immunoassay].

Time (illustrative)

Hands-on 10–15 minaliquot + internal standard + extraction
Instrument ~15–45 min/runHPLC gradient + MS dwell (batched)
Elapsed turnaround 1–10 daysbatch + QC + review; LabCorp lists 4–10 days for fractionated D [LabCorp 504115]

Not every D test takes days because it is slow chemistry; many are batched in reference labs and shipped. In-house immunoassay Ds can be same-day where offered.

Cutaway — LC-MS/MS: from serum to separated peaks to mass peaks

① Extraction & internal standard

Tech aliquots serum, adds deuterated d6-25-OH-D3 and d3-D2 as internal standards (same chemistry, heavier mass → distinct m/z), then protein-precipitates and liquid-liquid extracts with heptane/hexane. Why: vitamin D is protein-bound (DBP); we free it and correct for recovery ion suppression.

② HPLC — separates by chemistry, not mass

Autosampler injects extract onto a C18 reversed-phase column. Hydrophobic D2, D3 and 3-epi-25-OH-D3 partition into the stationary phase differently; gradient of methanol/water elutes them at different retention times — this is how LC-MS/MS resolves D2 vs D3 and the epimer that confounds immunoassays.

Chromatogram (representative): x = retention time (min), y = ion count. IS co-elutes with analyte — ratio corrects.

③ Tandem MS — quadrupole mass filter to photon-counted signal

Electrospray ionizes molecules. Q1 selects precursor m/z (e.g., 25-OH-D3 ≈ 401.3, D2 ≈ 413.3), collision cell fragments, Q3 selects product ion. Only that precursor→product transition is counted — isotope internal standard has same transition + mass shift. Peak area ratio (analyte/IS) → concentration via calibration curve.

No machine model is implied — any validated LC-MS/MS with deuterated IS and C18 separation that resolves 3-epi-25-OH-D3 operates on this principle [UW heptane extraction + LC-MS/MS].

Representative alternative — chemiluminescent immunoassay (same tube, different bench)

Competitive: anti-25-OH-D antibody + acridinium-labeled D compete for limited binding sites. Light output is inverse to patient D — more D = less label bound = less light. Fast, single total (not D2/D3 split), more epimer cross-reactivity. Some labs run this for routine volume; reference confirmation still LC-MS/MS.

Calibration & controls: 6-point calibrators (e.g., 5–120 ng/mL) + NIST SRM-traceable; two QC levels per batch; internal standard in every sample. Signal = area(analyte)/area(IS). Output: D2, D3 and total D2+D3 [Mayo 25HDN panel: D2, D3, total].

D2 12 ng/mL · D3 28 ng/mL → Total 40 ng/mL

D2-only from supplements; D3 from sun/animal. LC-MS/MS separates; immunoassay would lump them. Drug 3-epi contributes little here — resolved chromatographically.

TEST 02 / 11 · CLOT-BASED REFLEX · CITRATED PPP

Lupus Anticoagulant Reflex — PTT-LA and dRVVT as conditional branches

Lupus anticoagulant (LA) is a functional diagnosis: phospholipid-dependent autoantibodies that prolong phospholipid-limited clotting times in vitro but associate with thrombosis in vivo. Guidelines require at least two principles before exclusion — most labs pair LA-sensitive aPTT (PTT-LA / silica clotting time) and dilute Russell viper venom time (dRVVT) [ISTH/ISLH 2020 guidance]. Reflex is conditional: mixing and confirmation only run if screen is prolonged [ARUP 3017009 reflex] [Mayo ALUPP algorithm]. Not every sample sees every step.

Specimen input (representative for this bench)

  • Container: citrate — 3.2% Na citrate blue-top, 1 part citrate : 9 parts blood; fill to line, gentle 3–6 inversions.
  • Prep: rapid double-spin to platelet-poor plasma (PPP, typically <10k platelets/µL); test within 4 h or freeze — platelets supply phospholipid that can mask LA.
  • Rejection examples: underfill (>10% excess citrate chelates Ca²⁺ → falsely prolonged), clotted, heparin line contamination.

Each test in this panel has its own collection; this is the prep for the reflex LA benches (02 & 03 clot arm), not a universal prep.

Method class

Electromagnetic mechanical / photo-optical clot detection + chromogenic anti-Xa rule-out [ARUP method: EM clot / chromogenic]. Representative analyzer: automated coagulometer with photo-optical 671 nm or steel-ball viscosity detection — no model implied.

Time (illustrative)

Hands-on ~15 minspin, aliquot, load
Instrument ~30 min–1 h per roundscreen → conditional incubation
Elapsed often same day; with reflex/mixing ~4–8 h; confirm batch may next dayQC + path review

Not every LA requires days — screens are fast; delays come from reflex add-on, batch confirm, transport, or repeat after 12-week persistence check per classification criteria.

Reflex tree — tap a screen result to see what reflexes actually run

Reagents & molecular logic

PTT-LASilica/cell activator + low phospholipid + Ca²⁺. Intrinsic arm must assemble tenase/prothrombinase on anionic PL surface — LA antibody hides that surface → prolonged ratio.

dRVVT screen / confirmRussell viper venom RVV-X directly activates FX → FXa (bypasses contact/TF) [Practical Haemostasis: DRVVT]. Low PL (screen) sensitive; high PL (confirm) bypasses antibody. Ratio screen/confirm > cutoff suggests phospholipid dependence.

Mix 1:1 NPP50% normal pooled plasma supplies 50% of every factor — deficiency corrects to within ref; LA inhibitor stays in mix → persists.

Hexagonal phaseHexagonal phospholipid (HPE) in high hexagonal geometry avidly neutralizes LA — delta time or % correction quantifies phospholipid dependence. [OHSU: hexagonal neutralisation]

Calibration/QC: ratios normalize to pooled normal plasma run same day; daily QC normal/abnormal LA-sensitive controls; ISI-like standardization is PT-specific, not used here — each lab validates its own cutoffs per CLSI H60.

TEST 03 / 11 · APS PANEL: CLOT + IMMUNO · SERUM + PPP

Antiphospholipid Syndrome Panel — clotting times plus immune capture

APS criteria combine clinical (thrombosis / obstetric morbidity) with laboratory persistence of LA, anticardiolipin (aCL) or anti-β2-glycoprotein I (aβ2GP1) [ARUP APS algorithm]. This panel therefore runs the LA clot arm (bench 02) and two solid-phase immunoassay arms on serum — each answers a different question: does plasma clot phospholipid-dependently? Do serum antibodies bind cardiolipin or β2GP1?

Specimen inputs (representative)

  • Citrate PPP (blue-top aliquot, frozen): aPTT, PT, INR, thrombin time, hexagonal phase assay — same prep as bench 02 (double-spin PPP).
  • Serum (red/SST, fresh or frozen): aCL IgG/IgM & aβ2GP1 IgG/IgM by ELISA/chemiluminescence — separate aliquot, not citrate. Order set includes serum [Mayo: recommend separate serum for aPL].
  • Not: EDTA whole blood — that stays for DNA bench 05.

Methods in this composite

Clot-based aPTT (silica + low PL + Ca²⁺, 671 nm), PT (TF+PL+Ca²⁺, INR via ISI), thrombin time (bovine/human thrombin→ fibrin), hexagonal phase LA confirm — [ARUP LA method: EM/chromogenic] · Semi-quant ELISA / CIA aCL IgG/IgM & aβ2GP1 IgG/IgM on microplates with cardiolipin-β2GP1 or β2GP1 coated wells [ARUP aCL ELISA] [ARUP β2GP1 ELISA].

Time (illustrative — two benches run in parallel)

Hands-on ~20 mintwo aliquots, plate washer priming
Instrument ~1 h clot arm + 3–5 h ELISA arm (incubations)ELISA batch incubation dominates
Elapsed same-day to 2–5 dELISA batch + referral; persistence requires 12-week repeat

Two arms side-by-side — tap a panel component

Immuno arm — do antibodies bind?

PT/INR uses ISI/INR standardization for VKA monitoring; LA does not use ISI — ratios are lab-specific [Mayo PTSC ISI/INR]. TT uses exogenous thrombin, no Ca²⁺ needed.

TEST 04 / 11 · IMMUNOASSAY — ANTIGEN (QUANTITY) · PPP

Protein S Antigen — total vs free (what’s there, not what it does)

Protein S (PROS1) is a vitamin K-dependent anticoagulant cofactor for activated protein C and TFPI. In plasma ~60% circulates bound to C4b-binding protein (C4bBP) and inactive; ~40% is free and active. Total Protein S antigen measures both pools; free Protein S antigen measures only the free active fraction [Mayo PSF: 60% bound / 40% free] [ARUP 0098894 free]. Antigen ≠ function — function is bench 06.

Specimen input (representative)

  • Container: citrate PPP — blue-top 3.2% Na citrate, 1:9, double-spin to platelet-poor.
  • Aliquot: frozen citrate plasma — 0.5–1 mL typical for antigen panel; reflex adds total if free is low [ARUP reflex: free → total].
  • Not: serum (vit D/aPL tube) or EDTA whole blood (DNA) — different draws, different prep.

Method class

Microlatex particle-mediated immunoassay (representative) [ARUP: microlatex for free PS] or ELISA sandwich where lab’s platform varies — we label “representative” and show latex turbidity, the other common mode. Measures quantity (mg/dL or % of normal), not clotting time.

Time (illustrative)

Hands-on ~10 minthaw, dilute, load
Instrument ~30–60 minincubation + wash + turbidimetric read
Elapsed same-day batch; reflex total adds ~1 dayQC + review

Cutaway — free vs total: the C4bBP-bound fraction is hidden from function but not from total antigen

Signal: antibody-coated latex beads bridge via PS antigen → aggregates scatter light (nephelometry/turbidimetry at ~540–800 nm). More antigen = larger Δ absorbance. Calibration: pooled normal plasma assigned 100% free PS; 5–6 calibrator levels; QC normal/low. Result as % of normal or mg/L — units must be checked (not interchangeable without lab conversion).

Free PS 68% · Total 92% → pattern: low free, normal total (Type III-like acquired)

Estrogen ↑ C4bBP → more PS bound → free falls while total stays normal — acquired dip that mimics inherited Type III pattern. Interpretation needs context (age, sex, pregnancy, inflammation).

TEST 05 / 11 · DNA-BASED · WHOLE BLOOD

Factor V Leiden Mutation — reading G1691A, not timing a clot

Factor V Leiden is a single-base substitution in F5 (c.1691G>A, p.Arg506Gln) that removes an activated protein C cleavage site — factor Va resists inactivation. Labs detect the letter, not the clotting time: targeted genotyping by polymerase chain reaction (PCR) with allele-specific probes / melt or sequencing [ARUP F5 Leiden PCR] [LabCorp FVL].

Specimen input (representative)

  • Container: EDTA whole blood (lavender) — unspun, or buccal swab/saliva where validated. Not serum/plasma (those are acellular).
  • Why different: DNA comes from leukocytes; clot/serum has no cells; citrate plasma has no cells. Different question, different tube.
  • Stability: refrigerated days to weeks; PCR tolerates heparin poorly (EDTA preferred) — varies by lab.

Method class

Allele-specific PCR / melt / targeted genotyping — DNA extracted (spin column/beads), amplified with F5 exon 10 primers flanking 1691, detected with wild-type (G) and mutant (A) probes. No clotting reagent involved.

Time (illustrative)

Hands-on ~15 minextract, master mix
Instrument ~90–150 minthermocycling + melt
Elapsed 1–5 daysbatch + QC; reflex from APC resistance adds time

Cutaway — DNA to answer: thermocycler + melt discrimination

Real-time amplification — which probe lights?

Melt / allelic discrimination — genotype by peak(s)

Signal: fluorophore released on probe cleavage (TaqMan) or melt peak shift (FRET). Wild-type melt ~65 °C, mutant ~58 °C — illustrative, lab-validated. Controls: known G/G, G/A, A/A and no-template control each run. Variant reported as c.1691G>A genotype per HGVS.

TEST 06 / 11 · CLOT-BASED FUNCTIONAL · PPP · APC COFACTOR

Protein S Functional — the APC cofactor’s stopwatch

Functional Protein S measures whether your free PS actually helps activated protein C (APC) shut down Va/VIIIa. Patient PPP is mixed into Protein S-deficient substrate plasma (all factors except PS supplied), then APC (or Protac-activated endogenous PC) is added and a clotting time is triggered — prolongation is proportional to PS function [ARUP PS Functional: EM clot] [Mayo SFX: PT-based APC].

Specimen input (representative)

  • Container: citrate PPP — blue-top 1:9, double-spin. Functional assays are exquisitely sensitive to residual platelets and to lipemia.
  • Interferences (this bench only): acute thrombosis, pregnancy, estrogen, warfarin/Vit K antagonists ↓, acute-phase ↑FVIII/LA, FVL APC resistance — all falsely mimic or mask.

Method class

Clot-based APC cofactor assay — PT- or aPTT-based trigger [ARUP EM mechanical] vs Mayo PT-based recombinant TF + PL + APC [Mayo SFX]. Representative instrument: automated coagulometer (mechanical/optical).

Time (illustrative)

Hands-on ~10 minmix substrate + sample
Instrument ~30 minincubate + trigger + detect
Elapsed same-day to 1 dQC; often batched with AT/PC

Cutaway — substrate + patient → time prolongs only if PS works

Why functional is fragile: FVL (APC resistance), lupus anticoagulant, high FVIII, and heparins/DOACs all alter clotting times independently of PS — free antigen (bench 04) is the preferred screen for that reason [ARUP: preferred free PS antigen]. Calibration: pooled normal plasma dilutions assigned 100% activity; patient time interpolated on log-log curve; QC normal/abnormal.

82% — within illustrative normal (60–140%)

Short time = low PS function (less APC assistance); long time = high PS function. But if LA present, time also long for the wrong reason — assay flags may say “interference” rather than report.

TEST 07 / 11 · CHROMOGENIC ENZYMATIC ACTIVITY · PPP

Antithrombin Activity — heparin-cofactor chromogenic assay

Antithrombin (SERPINC1) is a heparin-activated serpin that traps thrombin (IIa) and FXa. Activity assays measure how well it inhibits, not how many molecules: plasma + excess heparin and fixed FXa (or IIa) → residual enzyme cleaves a chromogenic substrate to release yellow p-nitroaniline (pNA). More antithrombin = less residual enzyme = less color — an inverse readout [ARUP AT activity: chromogenic] [LabCorp AT: thrombin inhibition ∝ AT].

Specimen input (representative)

  • Container: citrate PPP — blue-top 1:9, double-spin. Antigen (quantity) vs activity (function) distinction matters — this bench is activity.
  • Stability: frozen citrate plasma typical; thaw once, assay prompt — lab-specific.

Method class

Chromogenic (representative: FXa-based) — heparin + bovine/human FXa or thrombin + chromogenic substrate (e.g., S-2765/S-2238). Some labs use anti-IIa variant; each has different heparin sensitivity — we label which is shown.

Time (illustrative)

Hands-on ~10 minthaw, heparin incubation
Instrument ~15 minchromogenic kinetic at 405 nm
Elapsed same-day batchoften batched with Proteins C/S

Cutaway — heparin makes antithrombin fast; leftover FXa makes color

Detection: kinetic absorbance at 405 nm; rate vs log(AT) calibration (human pooled normal = 100% activity ≈ 1 IU/mL). Controls: normal/deficient QC each run; heparin in sample spuriously raises apparent activity with FXa-based assays if not quenched — labs note therapy.

92% activity — illustrative normal (80–120%) → low residual FXa → low color rate

Type I deficiency: low molecules & activity. Type II: normal quantity, dysfunctional (e.g., heparin-binding or RS defect) — needs antigen (immunoassay) to subtype.

TEST 08 / 11 · IMMUNOASSAY ANTIGEN (QUANTITY) · PPP

Protein C Antigen — how many Protein C molecules, not how well they work

Protein C (PROC) is a vitamin K-dependent zymogen activated by thrombin-thrombomodulin to APC, which clips Va/VIIIa. Antigen assays count molecules by antibody capture; functional assays time activity (bench 10). Both needed to subtype type I (low quantity & low function) vs type II (normal quantity, low function) [ARUP PC antigen].

Specimen input (representative)

  • Container: citrate PPP — blue-top 1:9. Same tube as benches 02, 04, 06, 07, 09, 10 — but each assay’s prep has its own dilutions/buffers.
  • Anticoagulant sensitivity: warfarin lowers VK-dependent proteins (II, VII, IX, X, Protein C/S) — antigen will be low on warfarin for physiological reason, not inherited.

Method class

Sandwich ELISA / microlatex immunoassay (representative) — capture anti-Protein C + patient PC + HRP-labeled detector → TMB color. Some platforms use latex-enhanced immunoturbidimetry. We show ELISA washer cutaway — no model implied.

Time (illustrative)

Hands-on ~10 minplate setup
Instrument ~3–4 hincubate/wash/develop
Elapsed same-day to 1–2 d batchedreflex from low functional adds time

Cutaway — antibody sandwich captures Protein C

Calibration: human pooled normal plasma standards (e.g., 20–150% assigned by reference material); log(OD) vs log(conc) curve; QC high/low. Result as % of normal or mg/L (mass) — units lab-specific.

88% antigen — illustrative normal (70–140%)

Interpret antigen with functional: low/low = type I (low production); normal/low = type II (dysfunctional) — needs both benches to subtype [ARUP].

TEST 09 / 11 · CLOT-BASED ONE-STAGE ACTIVITY · PPP · PT-BASED

Factor II Activity — the prothrombin stopwatch (not a gene test)

Factor II activity measures functional prothrombin in plasma by how fast it clots when everything else is in excess. It is not prothrombin G20210A gene testing (that is a DNA test like bench 05). Method: patient PPP mixed with Factor II-deficient substrate plasma (normal plasma immunodepleted of FII), then PT reagent (tissue factor + phospholipid + Ca²⁺) triggers clotting; time to fibrin is compared to a calibration curve [Mayo F2: ACL TOP PT method] [LabCorp 086231: PT-based one-stage].

Specimen input (representative)

  • Container: citrate PPP — blue-top 1:9, double-spin. Same family as LA/proteins but assay-specific dilutions.
  • Pre-analytical: fill to line; test promptly or freeze. Heparin or direct thrombin/Xa inhibitors prolong — assay will read falsely low if drug present (note on report).
  • Distinction: Factor II activity (this bench, % activity) ≠ prothrombin G20210A gene variant (DNA, bench 05 analogue) — different specimen, different bench, different question.

Method class

One-stage PT-based clotting assay — substrate plasma supplies FV, VII, X, fibrinogen etc. in excess; only FII comes from patient dilution. Representative coagulometer: mechanical or optical clot detection.

Time (illustrative)

Hands-on ~10 mindilute 1:10, mix substrate
Instrument ~10–15 minPT trigger → fibrin time
Elapsed same-dayQC + review; inhibitor workup (Bethesda) would be add-on

Cutaway — deficient substrate makes patient FII rate-limiting

Calibration & controls: pooled normal plasma assigned 100% FII (≈1.0 IU/mL) diluted 1:10–1:80 vs time; log-log curve; patient % interpolated; QC normal/low [Mayo calibration]. Signal: fibrin formation time (mechanical/optical). Short time = high FII; long = low.

98% — illustrative normal (70–130% example)

Warfarin, vitamin K deficiency, liver disease, DIC consumption ↓; acute phase not raise much (FII stable). Direct oral anticoagulants prolong spuriously — note therapy.
