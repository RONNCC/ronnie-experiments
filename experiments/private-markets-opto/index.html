03 · PRIVATE MARKETS MAP

What “private” even means

Public markets = trade on an exchange, daily price, anyone can buy. Private markets = ownership negotiated off-exchange, priced quarterly by appraisal/model, locked up for years, restricted to eligible investors. Companies now stay private ~16 years to IPO — so growth that used to accrue to public shareholders increasingly accrues privately first. That's the demand story every RIA is hearing from clients.

Public (S&P 500, bonds)

  • Daily liquidity + transparent price
  • Low fees, instant diversification
  • Mark-to-market volatility you feel daily
  • Shrinking opportunity set (fewer listed cos)
vs

Private (PE, credit, VC…)

  • Illiquid 5–12 yrs; quarterly NAV, lagged
  • High fees (often 2% + 20% carry at GP level)
  • Smoother reported volatility (debated: real or appraisal lag?)
  • Access to pre-IPO growth, direct lending, real assets

04 · HOW A PRIVATE FUND ACTUALLY WORKS

Commit → call → invest → return: the 12-year machine

A drawdown fund is not “wire money, own assets.” Investors commit a promise; the GP calls cash over ~5 years as deals close; value builds; exits distribute cash back. Run the teaching simulator — then you'll never fumble “capital call” again. TEACHING MODEL

Step through years. Watch paid-in rise, NAV dip (J-curve), then distributions overtake.

Year 0 — Commitment signed

GP vs LP

GP (general partner) = the manager (e.g., a buyout firm). Picks deals, charges mgmt fee + carry (~20% over hurdle). LP (limited partner) = the investor. In Opto's world the RIA's custom fund is often one LP into many GP funds — Opto aggregates many end-clients into that single LP seat.

Vintage & pacing

Vintage = year a fund starts investing. You don't put 20% into privates in one year — you pace commitments across vintages so calls/distributions smooth out and you aren't concentrated in one market year. Opto's Planner tool models exactly this ramp.

Secondaries & continuation vehicles

LP-led: an LP sells its fund stake early (often ~90% of NAV). GP-led / CV: GP moves a prized asset into a new vehicle; old LPs cash out or roll. Secondaries hit ~$210B volume in 2025 — they're now core liquidity plumbing, not a distress signal. Ask how Opto diligence prices CV conflicts (same GP on both sides).

05 · METRICS LAB — SPEAK THE LANGUAGE

Four numbers run every LP conversation

Insiders judge a fund on DPI + RVPI = TVPI, plus IRR. Learn what each hides.

🧮 Try it: paid-in, distributions, NAV

DPI is cash reality (no estimate). TVPI is only as honest as the NAV inside it. Early “great TVPI, zero DPI” = marks, not money. LPs in 2025–26 gate re-ups on DPI.

🎯 Pacing calculator (RIA portfolio)

Rule-of-thumb teaching model: over-commit because capital is called gradually and distributions recycle. Real pacing models simulate call/distribution curves by strategy — that's the analytics Opto sells.

SOUND SENIOR “Early IRR mostly tells me about the J-curve and subscription-line timing, not skill. I'd want DPI by vintage and PME vs a public benchmark before I believed a track record.”

🕰 Teach-back: the two clocks (60 seconds)

Business time = when a fact was true in the world. System time = when Opto learned it. A late correction shouldn't overwrite history — you close the old system-version and insert a rebuilt business timeline per entity. Now you can answer both “what's true now?” and “what did we believe on decision day?” Tradeoff: write amplification + concurrency care.

ASK MATT “How do you resolve conflicting observations with the same effective date, and what makes a timeline rebuild atomic?”

🤖 Teach-back: man-machine symbiosis + Diligence AI

Opto's stated philosophy (Apr 2025 post): augment, don't replace the adviser/CIO/ops expert. Diligence AI turns data-room troves into screening memos in minutes so humans debate judgment, not PDFs. Fund-formation doc generator does the same for legal drafts. Your line: “AI for throughput, humans for accountability — with evals and permissions between them.”

Don't claim the ontology/feedback-learning vision (Aug 2025 “AI for finance”) is fully shipped. Ask which parts are production today.

09 · BUSINESS, MOAT & RISKS

The only chart Ryan ultimately cares about

Ryan's background — Quellos → BlackRock MD (APAC finance, iShares FP&A) → Opto CFO → CEO — means he'll evaluate engineering through economics and execution. Show you think in service-hours per fund. TEACHING MODEL

If hours/fund don't fall, headcount must grow linearly — the “deliberately small” thesis dies. Internal-tools team (Aug 2026) exists precisely to bend this curve.

🏰 Strongest moat argument

Embedded workflows + normalized bitemporal history + integration coverage + customer-specific programs → switching costs. Evidence of capability exists (temporal data, admin-neutral workflow). Proof at scale = retention + margin data you don't have — ask for it.

🗡 Best counter-argument

An incumbent (iCapital/CAIS/custodian) bundles “good enough” through an existing relationship, while bespoke support leaves Opto with services economics in software clothing. Large RIAs may also in-source (PitchBook, July 2026: wealth firms “ditch the middleman”).

🔍 Risks to diligence (ask, don't accuse)

  • Services intensity: human hrs per fund/investor trend?
  • Revenue timing: performance-weighted = lumpy cash vs SaaS smoothness?
  • Concentration: one top-5 bank could hijack the roadmap.
  • Correctness: one permissions/NAV error torpedoes fiduciary trust.
  • AI differentiation: extraction + memo-gen commoditize fast.
  • Breadth: external platform + internal ops + IM + proprietary funds, one small eng org.
