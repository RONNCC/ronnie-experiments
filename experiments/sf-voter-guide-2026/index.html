How to vote

Election-day logistics and a district-aware ballot map for the 94102 area.

Voting logistics

Election Day: Tuesday, November 3, 2026.

  • Polls at all 501 San Francisco polling places and the City Hall Voting Center are open 7:00 a.m.–8:00 p.m.
  • Vote-by-mail ballots must be postmarked on or before Nov. 3, or hand-delivered to any polling place, drop box, or City Hall by 8:00 p.m. Nov. 3. The pack lists 37 official 24/7 drop boxes citywide.
  • Same-day registration is available at City Hall Voting Center or any polling place through 8:00 p.m. on Election Day.
  • Track a ballot at https://WheresMyBallot.sos.ca.gov; find a polling place at https://sfelections.gov/myvotinglocation; check registration and districts at https://sfelections.gov/voterportal.

Your exact ballot — check before relying on district examples.

Southern slivers of 94102 may instead be in D6, CA-15, or AD-19. BART D8 appears only in some precincts. Check https://sfelections.gov/voterportal for your own precinct and contest list.

Ballot map · pack summary

What can appear on a 94102-area ballot?

The supplied pack calls this a four-card ballot, but it does not map contests to individual physical cards. This is a content map by contest type, not a card-by-card layout.

Voter-nominated (partisan)

Governor, Lieutenant Governor, Secretary of State, Controller, Treasurer, Attorney General, Insurance Commissioner, Board of Equalization District 2, U.S. Representative (CA-11 or CA-15), State Assembly (AD-17 or AD-19).

Nonpartisan offices & retentions

State Superintendent of Public Instruction; Board of Education (vote for up to 3); Community College Board full term (up to 3) and partial term (1); BART Director District 8 (some precincts); judicial retention questions (YES/NO).

Local ranked-choice offices

Assessor-Recorder; Public Defender; Supervisor only in even-numbered districts voting this year, including D6 for a possible 94102 sliver. D5 is not on the ballot this year.

Measures

14 California propositions (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 37–45 as listed in this guide), regional transit measure RTM, and 10 San Francisco measures A–J.

Also noted in the pack: State Senate SD-11 is not on this 2026 ballot; it next elects in 2028. Southern 94102 district boundaries are not inferred here—use the voter portal.

Moderate / pro-housing tech network

Governance measures & the slate

The pack connects the GrowSF/TogetherSF ecosystem with funding from Michael Moritz (Sequoia), John Pritzker, and Chris Larsen (Ripple), and says the network bankrolls YES on D/E/F and GrowSF's slate, including Wiener, Dorsey, Haney, Sherrill, and Wong. GrowUSA backs Wiener federally.

Interpretation of the relationships described in the pack.

AI companies in local politics

Prop H and CA-11

The pack names OpenAI, Anthropic, and Ripple Labs among top funders of YES on H, San Francisco's Muni parcel-tax measure. It also says Anthropic-backed Public First Action put $3M into pro-Wiener Abundant Future PAC.

The pack reports the connections; this guide does not infer a donor's intent.

Organized labor

Different alignments in CA-11

The pack links NUHW to YES on B and Wiener; it lists SF Labor Council, Building Trades, National Nurses United, United Educators of SF, Fire Fighters Local 798, and hotel workers among Chan's support; Families for an Affordable San Francisco is described as mostly union-funded and pro-Wiener; SF for All as labor-backed and pro-Chan.

Pelosi network

Endorsement & fundraising

The pack says Nancy Pelosi endorsed Chan and hosted fundraisers for her. It lists Pelosi among Chan's claimed endorsers. This is shown as a relationship, not as a policy position.

Progressive housing left

Props B and I

The pack describes YES on I as connected to a social-housing faction, naming Christin Evans, Dylan Hirsch-Shell, Charles F. Good Trust, and Dean Preston among YES-side contributors. It describes YES on B as a public-bank faction, with the San Francisco Foundation, Christin Evans, and NUHW among paid-argument funders.

Opposition funders

Prop I's NO side

The pack names Quentin L. Kopp, Peter Fatooh, and Gina Tse-Louie as NO-side funders for Prop I. The label “old-guard fiscal conservatives” is the pack's interpretation, not a formal group name.

Real-estate interests

Transfer taxes & parcel tax

The pack notes that Props I and J rewrite transfer-tax rules and says landlords and commercial real estate may watch H, I, and J closely. This is framed neutrally; no specific landlord or commercial-real-estate contributor is named in that note.

CA-11 money comparison

Outside spending

Mission Local figures reproduced in the pack report $3.3M in outside PAC spending supporting Wiener and $1.7M attacking Chan, versus $940k supporting Chan and $260k against Wiener. The pack characterizes the outside-spending advantage as roughly 5:1; this is its interpretation of the reported figures.
