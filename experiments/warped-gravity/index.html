SMALL MAGNET. WHOLE PLANET.

Why is gravity
so weak?

A magnet can lift a paper clip against the pull of the entire Earth. One possible explanation takes us into a hidden direction of space.

Start with what you can hold. Build the picture one idea at a time. You do not need any physics or math.

What kind of story is this? Gravity's weakness is real. The hidden dimension is a hypothesis: an idea scientists can test, not something we know exists.

FOLLOW THE EVIDENCE

Where this comes from

This is a teaching visualization, not a numerical solution of five-dimensional gravity and not evidence for an extra dimension. The warp lab evaluates an exponential exactly; its drawings compress several dimensions into a picture.

  1. Randall & Sundrum, A Large Mass Hierarchy from a Small Extra Dimension (1999)

    The original two-brane proposal. Equations 12, 16, 20 and 21 connect geometry, gravity and particle mass scales. Read the full paper.

  2. CERN, Warped Extra Dimensions and the Randall-Sundrum Model (2006)

    Expert review of the geometric rescaling and the two-brane setup. The separation parameter k rc is roughly 11–12 for the hierarchy example.

  3. Particle Data Group, Extra Dimensions (2020 review)

    Warped models, other extra-dimension ideas and experimental constraints. Old numerical limits are not treated as today's limits here.

  4. CERN, Extra dimensions, gravitons, and tiny black holes

    The magnet and wire analogies. This page discusses several ideas, not only the Randall-Sundrum model.

  5. Harvard Physics, Lisa Randall

    Her research, books and opera libretto.

  6. The New Yorker, The Dark Energy of a Theoretical Physicist (2016)

    Biography and tenure milestones. First tenured woman in Princeton physics; first tenured female theoretical physicist at Harvard.

  7. Randall & Sundrum, An Alternative to Compactification (1999)

    The related one-brane model, often called RS2. It asks a different question from the two-brane hierarchy model here.

  8. MIT OpenCourseWare, electric versus gravitational force

    A worked electron–proton comparison gives about 2 × 1039, showing why 1032 is not a universal force ratio.
