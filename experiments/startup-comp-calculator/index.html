      The math

      How each number is computed

      1. Ownership

        your shares ÷ fully diluted shares

        A dollar value grant is converted at the implied preferred price, post money ÷ fully diluted shares. Check which price your offer letter used.

      2. Dilution per round

        raise ÷ post money + pool top up

        New investors buy raise ÷ post money of the company. A pool top up placed in the pre money dilutes existing holders only. You keep one minus both.

      3. Preference stack

        raised to date + Σ raise × multiple

        All series are paid pari passu, side by side. Nonparticipating series convert to common when converting pays more than their preference.

      4. Per common share

        (exit − unconverted preferences) ÷ (common + converted preferred)

        The unissued pool counts as common, the fully diluted view. That slightly understates value if part of the pool is never granted.

      5. Your payout

        vested shares × max(0, per share − strike)

        RSUs ignore the strike. Vesting stops when you leave, so a short stay caps the vested shares even if the exit comes later.

      6. Break even exit

        smallest exit where payout ≥ salary gap × years you stay

        Found by bisection, which works because your payout never falls as the exit value rises. All figures are before tax.

      Before you sign

      Questions that fill in this calculator

      Companies often answer these on request. Without the first two, a share count tells you almost nothing.

      1. How many fully diluted shares are outstanding?

        Ask whether the figure includes the full option pool, warrants, and convertible notes or SAFEs that have not converted yet.

      2. What was the price per share in the last round, and what is the current 409A?

        The preferred price shows what investors paid. The 409A sets your strike and the tax you owe if you exercise early.

      3. How much preference is ahead of common, and is any of it participating?

        Total raised is a starting estimate. Multiples above 1x or participation make the flat part of the payout curve longer.

      4. How long do I have to exercise after leaving?

        A short window can force a large purchase, and possibly a tax bill, within weeks of leaving. Some companies offer longer windows.

      5. Can I exercise early, before shares vest?

        Early exercise with an 83(b) election can start the capital gains clock sooner. It also puts your own money at risk. Talk to a tax advisor first.

      6. What happens to unvested shares if the company is acquired?

        Single or double trigger acceleration decides whether unvested shares vest at a sale. This calculator assumes no acceleration.

      Glossary

      Terms in plain language

      Fully diluted shares
      Every share that exists or could exist: common, preferred, granted options, and the unissued option pool.
      Post money valuation
      The company's price right after a round closes: pre money plus the cash raised.
      Preferred stock
      What investors usually buy. It carries a liquidation preference and other rights common stock lacks.
      Liquidation preference
      The amount preferred holders get back before common in a sale, usually 1x what they invested.
      Participating
      A preference term where investors take their money back and also share in the rest as if converted.
      Option pool top up
      New shares reserved for future hires, usually created in the pre money so only existing holders are diluted.
      Stock option
      The right to buy a share at a fixed strike price. Worth the spread between share value and strike.
      RSU
      Restricted stock unit. A promise of a share with no purchase price, usually taxed as income when it settles.
      409A valuation
      An independent appraisal of common stock that sets option strike prices for US tax purposes.
      Cliff
      The service period before any shares vest. With a one year cliff, a quarter of a four year grant vests at once at month twelve.
      Pari passu
      Paid side by side at equal priority, in proportion to each claim.
      Exercise window
      How long you have after leaving to buy your vested options before they expire.
