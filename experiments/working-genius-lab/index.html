Why 24 questions, and how scoring works

Not a 45-question replica

The official Working Genius FAQ describes 42 statements. Its publisher also documents tie-breakers, but the public sources do not establish a fixed total of 45. This independent experiment keeps 24 original items, four per domain, as a short-form pilot. More items alone would not establish reliability or validity. A longer form should earn its length through content review and real participant testing.

Transparent descriptive scoring

Each available numeric answer has equal weight. We divide their sum by their count within a domain. The 1-5 categories are treated as equally spaced for this arithmetic summary, not as a calibrated psychological scale. No reverse scoring, hidden weights, centering, percentiles or overall total.

Not experienced and too variable are unscored, never replaced by neutral. With three numeric answers we show a partial-coverage mean; fewer than three produces no score. This minimum is a product convention, not a validated reliability threshold. Missing activities may change the picture.

Equal unrounded means stay tied. We flag pairs within 0.25 points as close, without chaining them into groups. A one-step change to one of four answers moves a mean by 0.25; with three it moves by about 0.33. This is a display convention, not a confidence interval or proof that larger gaps matter. Two decimal places show arithmetic, not psychological precision.

All six results remain visible. Above or below 3 describes only the neutral response anchor, not a validated cutoff. We do not force two genius, two competency and two frustration labels: energy ratings cannot establish competence, and even the lowest score can be energizing. Independent ratings are not a forced-choice or true ipsative test.

What would make it a better instrument?

Define domain boundaries and facets; have subject experts review coverage; run cognitive interviews with intended users; pilot a larger original item pool; check item distributions, missingness, six-domain structure, internal consistency per domain, test-retest stability and fairness across groups; confirm findings in an independent sample. None of that participant validation has been done here. Software tests only verify calculations and behavior.

No established reliability, validity, norms or equivalence to the official assessment. Do not use for hiring, performance evaluation or diagnosis. Version 2 changes item wording and reporting; old version-1 links remain readable but are not directly comparable.

A plain-language glossary

Six types describe activities. Three categories describe a person's relationship to those activities.

Keep the model in perspective.

Being drained by an activity does not make you bad at it. Getting energy from an activity does not prove you do it well. Roles, rest, team support and practice still matter. Try noticing your energy over several actual projects before changing how you work.
